Record each expense once, use a small set of categories that affect your decisions, and reconcile account balances at a fixed time. A two-minute daily capture plus a ten-minute weekly review is more reliable than reconstructing an entire month from memory.

Set up accounts and categories

Create only the accounts you currently use: perhaps Cash, Bank, E-wallet, and Credit Card. Enter the starting balance immediately before your first recorded transaction. Begin with five to eight broad categories such as food, transport, housing, bills, health, and leisure.

Categories should help a decision. If splitting coffee from meals will not change anything, keep both under Food. You can add detail later.

Follow a five-minute daily routine

  1. Record purchases when possible. Add the amount, account, category, and a recognizable note.
  2. Complete the remaining entries once. Use receipts and payment histories at the same time every evening.
  3. Record transfers correctly. Moving money between your own accounts is not spending.
  4. Check the resulting balance. A mismatch is easier to find before receipts or transaction details are forgotten.
  5. Stop when reconciled. Do not turn daily capture into analysis.

A tracker such as Squirio keeps manual entries fast and works offline. If several weeks already exist in a statement, use the CSV import workflow instead of typing every row.

Review weekly, decide monthly

Once a week, find uncategorized items, duplicates, and balance differences. At the end of the budget period, compare actual spending with the plan. Focus on one or two changes rather than trying to optimize everything.
